A sample’s location in \(n\)-dimensional space is defined by its position on each of the \(n\) available axes. These positions are called scores and a sample’s scores change in value as we rotate the axes. The figure on the right shows a data set that has eight samples (the black circles) and two variables (the magenta and light blue lines). The small number of samples ensures the figure is uncluttered, so individual points are easy to see, and using just two variables allows you to fully view the data set within your screen’s limit of two-dimensions. A slider allows you to rotate the axes in space, with the angle of rotation defined relative to the original axes. As a visual reference, when you rotate the axes, the original axes are preserved and displayed as dashed lines.
