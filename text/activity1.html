The figure on the right shows 205 points—in light blue—drawn at random from within the boundaries of an ellipsoid that has a length, \(x\), of 30, a width, \(y\), of 18, and a height, \(z\), of 4, centered at \(x = y = z = 0\). The data are viewed in three dimensions, defined by each point’s position along the \(x\)-axis (in orange), the \(y\)-axis (in green), and the \(z\)-axis, in magenta. These are the original measurement axes.

Exercise 1. There are a number of controls you can use to examine this data: hover the cursor within the plot to revel the icons for these controls. There are icons that pan and zoom the data, and a camera icon that saves the current display as a .png file. There are two icons—orbital and turntable—that rotate the data in space. Play with these controls until you feel comfortable with how your movements affect how you see the data. Document your work using the camera icon. Click the home icon to restore the data to its original view.

Exercise 2. Click on one of the axis icon’s in the legend and note that doing so removes the corresponding axis from the plot. Remove all three axes—leaving just the data points—and continue to examine the data by clicking, dragging, and rotating the data. Document your work using the camera icon.

Exercise 3. When you are comfortable with the controls, add back the axes and describe the distribution of the data points relative to the \(x\)-axis, the \(y\)-axis, and/or the \(z\)-axis. Are the data points aligned closely with one of these axes or between pairs of axes? Or, is there no discernible pattern in the data’s distribution relative to the axes? Document your work using the camera icon.

Exercise 4. Remove the three axes and describe the shape of the data without regard to the \(x\)-axis, the \(y\)-axis, and the \(z\)-axis. Explain why this shape is consistent with your description of the ellipsoid in Exercise 3. Document your work using the camera icon.
