The data on the previous tab are not aligned with the \(x\)-, \(y\)-, and \(z\)-axes, which means the original axes are not the simplest model of the data. The goal of a principal component analysis is to find a new set of axes that best model the data. To find these axes we rotate the original axes in space. If we begin with three mutually orthogonal axes then we will end with three new axes that are mutually orthogonal.

Exercise 1. The first principal component axis, PC1, accounts for the most variability in the data. Each subsequent principal component axis accounts for the next most variability in the data. When you compare two axes, how might you decide which accounts for more of the data’s variability?
